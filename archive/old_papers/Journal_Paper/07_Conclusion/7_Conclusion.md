# 7. Conclusion

This work addressed boundary fidelity in dermoscopic skin-lesion segmentation, where overlap-oriented training tends to produce smooth predictions that misrepresent the lesion margin, the region on which dermoscopic diagnosis most depends. Rather than introducing a new architecture, we built on MAFF-Net. We first reconstructed its components, namely a frozen SAM2 image encoder, the Mixed Multi-scale Perception Adaptor, the Frequency-Guided Feature Fusion module, and the auxiliary Reconstruction Decoder Block, into a faithful and reproducible base with numerically stable spectral computation. This restoration is treated as the experimental base and not as a contribution, and we keep it distinct from the original published model throughout.

On this base we proposed Boundary-centric Dual Supervision, which constitutes our contribution. It derives a single boundary prior from the ground-truth mask and applies it in two complementary ways. Boundary-aware Deep Supervision adds an edge-agreement signal at the decoder's multi-scale predictions, and Edge-weighted Reconstruction reweights the auxiliary reconstruction toward boundary regions. Both terms act only on the training objective, so they introduce no parameters and leave the inference-time model identical to the restored base, and setting their weights to zero recovers the restored baseline. The contribution is therefore a coherent, low-overhead supervision strategy rather than an architectural change, and it is designed so that its effect can be isolated by ablation.

We evaluated the restored base and the proposed supervision on the ISIC 2016, ISIC 2017, and ISIC 2018 benchmarks against U-Net, Attention U-Net, UNet++, and TransUNet, with each component examined through a controlled ablation under a uniform training protocol. *[Summary of the quantitative findings to be inserted from Section 5, covering the effect of Boundary-aware Deep Supervision, Edge-weighted Reconstruction, and their combination relative to the restored base.]* We report these outcomes without claiming state-of-the-art performance and without overstating the scope of the contribution, which is confined to the supervision strategy.

Several directions remain open. Boundary-specific evaluation metrics would allow the margin behaviour of the method to be measured directly rather than inferred from overlap metrics and qualitative comparison. A fuller study of the supervision weights and of the boundary prior would clarify how sensitive the method is to its hyperparameters. Evaluation on additional datasets and imaging modalities would test whether the benefit observed on dermoscopic images transfers more broadly. Finally, because the supervision depends only on a boundary prior together with deep supervision and an auxiliary reconstruction path, it could in principle be applied to other architectures that provide these; we note this as a direction for future work rather than a claim of the present study.
