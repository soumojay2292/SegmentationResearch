## 3.3 Frequency-Guided Feature Fusion (FGFF)

The Frequency-Guided Feature Fusion module is a component of the original MAFF-Net and is reproduced in the restored base; it is not part of the proposed contribution. Its purpose follows directly from the limitation discussed earlier: repeated downsampling in the encoder attenuates the high-frequency content that carries edge information, and a purely spatial decoder cannot recover what the encoder has suppressed. FGFF addresses this by giving the network a second, frequency-domain view of the same features, so that the boundary information weakened in the spatial path can be reintroduced before decoding. The module operates at each scale of the adapted feature hierarchy and returns a feature of the same shape, leaving the rest of the architecture unchanged.

The frequency view is obtained with the two-dimensional fast Fourier transform. For an adapted feature map, the transform produces a complex spectrum, of which only the magnitude is retained; the phase is discarded. The reasoning, following the original design, is that the magnitude spectrum reflects the distribution of texture and edge energy and is informative for lesion structure, whereas the phase mainly encodes spatial position and is easily confounded with the position of artifacts such as hair and vessels. The retained magnitude is normalized to a common range so that its scale is comparable across feature maps, and it is resized to match the spatial resolution of the corresponding feature so that the two views can be combined point by point. To keep this step numerically stable, the transform is computed in single precision within the otherwise mixed-precision pipeline.

Fusion combines a spatial representation with the frequency-derived signal. On the spatial side, the feature is passed through a set of parallel dilated convolutions with different dilation rates and then merged, which gathers context over several receptive fields and yields a multi-scale spatial feature $F_{s}$. On the frequency side, the normalized magnitude is summarized across channels by average and maximum pooling and mapped through a convolution and a sigmoid into a spatial attention map $A \in [0,1]$, which indicates where frequency evidence is strong. The two views are then merged so that the frequency attention modulates the spatial feature, weighted by a learnable per-channel coefficient $\beta$,

$$F_{\text{out}} = \beta \odot \big(A \otimes F_{s}\big),$$

where $\otimes$ denotes spatially broadcast multiplication and $\odot$ channel-wise scaling, and the result is combined with the input through a residual connection. In this form the frequency information acts as a gate that emphasizes the spatial response at locations with strong edge energy, rather than being concatenated as an independent stream, and $\beta$ lets the network learn how much weight the frequency cue should carry in each channel.

Within MAFF-Net, FGFF sits between the per-scale adaptation produced by the MMPA and the decoder. It supplies the decoder with features in which boundary-frequency content has been restored and aligned with the spatial representation, which is the property the frequency-guided design is intended to provide. The restored base reproduces this behaviour, and the supervision introduced in the following subsections operates on the network's outputs without modifying the fusion module.
