## 3.5 Edge-weighted Reconstruction Loss

This subsection introduces the second part of our contribution, Edge-weighted Reconstruction (EWR), which also extends the restored MAFF-Net. As with the boundary supervision of the previous subsection, EWR changes only the training objective and leaves the network unchanged. The reconstruction branch it acts on is used during training and removed at inference, so EWR adds no cost to the deployed model.

The restored model retains MAFF-Net's auxiliary reconstruction branch, which reconstructs the input image from the encoder features and is trained with a reconstruction loss. In its original form this loss is a plain mean-squared error that compares the reconstruction with the input at every pixel with equal weight. Such a loss rewards faithful global appearance, but because the lesion interior and the background occupy most of the image, the reconstruction is driven mainly by these large, low-detail regions. The boundary, which carries the fine structure most relevant to segmentation, contributes little to the loss and is reconstructed only incidentally. EWR redirects this objective so that the reconstruction branch becomes responsible for the regions that matter for the lesion margin.

The redirection is achieved by weighting the reconstruction error spatially according to proximity to the lesion boundary. We reuse the boundary prior already defined for the segmentation supervision, $B(y) = S(y)$, the Sobel response of the ground-truth mask, optionally widened into a band, so that the two parts of the contribution are anchored to the same information. From this prior we form a weight map

$$W(y) = 1 + \alpha\, B(y),$$

where $\alpha \geq 0$ controls how strongly boundary regions are emphasized. The constant term keeps the weight at one everywhere, so the interior is still reconstructed, while the boundary receives proportionally more weight. Writing $x$ for the input image and $r$ for the reconstruction mapped to the same range, the edge-weighted reconstruction loss is the normalized weighted mean-squared error

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{EWR}} = \frac{\sum W(y)\,(r - x)^2}{C \sum W(y)},$$

where the sums run over spatial positions and the batch, $C$ is the number of channels, and the normalization by $\sum W(y)$ keeps the loss scale independent of $\alpha$ and of the amount of boundary present in a given image. Setting $\alpha = 0$ recovers the original uniform reconstruction loss, so the change is a strict generalization of the restored model's behaviour.

EWR is attached to the existing reconstruction branch without altering its structure, and it is combined with the rest of the objective through a single weight. Together with the boundary supervision of the previous subsection, the complete training objective of the proposed framework is

$$\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{seg}} + \lambda_b(t)\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{BDS}} + \lambda_r\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{EWR}},$$

where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{seg}}$ is the restored model's segmentation loss, $\lambda_b(t)$ follows the warmup schedule defined earlier, and $\lambda_r$ weights the reconstruction term. Because $\mathcal{L}_{\text{BDS}}$ and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{EWR}}$ are both derived from the same boundary prior $B(y)$ but applied to two different parts of the network, the segmentation outputs and the reconstruction output, they supervise the lesion boundary from two complementary directions within a single training pass. This pairing is what we refer to as Boundary-centric Dual Supervision.

By steering the auxiliary reconstruction toward boundary regions, EWR is intended to encourage the shared encoder features to preserve margin detail rather than only global appearance, reinforcing the boundary signal that the segmentation heads receive. As with the boundary supervision, the contribution of this term is examined in the ablation study rather than claimed in advance.
