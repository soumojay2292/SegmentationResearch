VI-A. Interpretation of the Component-wise Observations

The ablation reported in Section V-C establishes that disabling any one of the three components introduced in this work degrades all three boundary measures simultaneously, while leaving the region measures essentially undisturbed. The experiments determine that this association exists; they do not, by themselves, identify the mechanism responsible for it, since no analysis of the intermediate representations was undertaken. The accounts offered below are therefore interpretations consistent with the design described in Section III and with the observed behaviour, and they are advanced as such rather than as demonstrated explanations.

The Frequency Branch applies a fixed radial high-pass filter to each encoder feature and returns the retained content to the spatial domain. Intensity transitions at the lesion margin are among the structures represented in the higher-frequency portion of the spectrum, and a plausible reading of the ablation is that retaining an explicit route for this content preserves margin-associated information that would otherwise be carried in a single representation together with the lower-frequency content describing the comparatively uniform lesion interior. One aspect of this comparison is unusually clean and merits emphasis: the module contains no trainable parameters, so replacing it with an identity mapping leaves the trainable parameter count of the network unchanged. The degradation in the boundary measures that follows its removal therefore cannot be attributed to a reduction in model capacity, and is attributable to the spectral operation itself. This does not establish which spectral content is responsible, only that the operation, and not the capacity, accounts for the difference.

Removing the Feature Alignment Module produced the largest degradation in the average symmetric surface distance and in the Boundary IoU of the three ablations. The two streams reaching the fusion stage are of different provenance: the spatial stream carries the full content of the encoder feature, whereas the frequency stream, having had its low-frequency components suppressed, is approximately zero-mean and occupies a different range of values. A reasonable interpretation is that combining representations of dissimilar offset and scale permits one stream to dominate the combination, and that normalising both onto a common footing before the gate is computed allows the subsequent weighting to reflect the content of the two streams rather than their relative magnitudes. This interpretation carries an important qualification. The module comprises a normalisation together with a learned projection and a nonlinearity, and disabling it removes all three; the ablated configuration therefore has slightly fewer trainable parameters than the complete model. The observed degradation cannot be separated, on the present evidence, into the contribution of the normalisation and that of the reduced capacity.

Replacing the Gated Fusion Module with a fixed unweighted mean produced the largest increase in the 95th-percentile Hausdorff distance of the three ablations, while producing the smallest changes in the average symmetric surface distance and the Boundary IoU. A fixed mean applies the same weighting to the two streams at every position and for every input, whereas the gate is computed from the streams themselves and may vary across channels and spatial positions. If the relative usefulness of contextual and margin-associated content differs between the lesion interior and the neighbourhood of the margin, a position-dependent weighting is able to reflect that difference where a fixed rule cannot. That the effect of this module is most pronounced on the 95th-percentile distance, a measure governed by the larger contour deviations, and least pronounced on the mean surface distance may indicate that its influence is concentrated on the more severe departures from the reference contour rather than on the typical ones; this remains a conjecture that the present experiments do not test. Here too the ablated configuration has marginally fewer trainable parameters than the complete model.

VI-B. Boundary Fidelity versus Region Overlap

That the improvements associated with these components appear in the boundary measures and not in the Dice coefficient follows from a geometric property of the measures themselves rather than from any peculiarity of the models. Region overlap is an area-based quantity, and the neighbourhood of the contour occupies a small fraction of the area of a compact lesion. Displacing a contour by a few pixels alters the overlapping area only slightly, and the resulting change in the Dice coefficient is correspondingly small; the same displacement enters a contour-distance measure directly and in full. A modification that acts principally on the placement of the margin may therefore leave region overlap almost unchanged while producing a measurable change in the boundary measures. The observations of Section V-C are consistent with this account: the region measures varied within a narrow band across the four configurations, and the complete model did not record the highest value on any of them, whereas the boundary measures separated the configurations consistently.

Two consequences follow for the interpretation of this work. The first is that the contribution of the three components is properly characterised as an improvement in the fidelity of the predicted contour, and not as an improvement in region-overlap performance; the evidence does not support the latter and no such claim is made. The second is that region-overlap measures alone are insufficient to characterise the behaviour of methods designed to act on the lesion margin, a point for which Section V-B provides independent support in the disagreement between the orderings induced by the two families of measures. This second observation is methodological rather than architectural, and applies equally to the evaluation of other boundary-oriented methods. Both consequences should nonetheless be weighed against the magnitudes involved, which are small in relation to the per-image dispersion of the distance measures.

VI-C. Retaining the Frozen Foundation Backbone

Retaining the pretrained encoder as a fixed feature extractor carries several advantages in this setting. The representations it provides are learned from a volume and diversity of data that the available dermoscopic collections do not approach, which is material given the size of the datasets employed here. Because the encoder is not updated, the trainable capacity of the network is concentrated in the modules that operate downstream of it and constitutes a small fraction of the total parameter count, which reduces the number of parameters that must be estimated from the comparatively limited annotated data available.

A further advantage is methodological and bears directly on the interpretation of the results. Because the same frozen encoder is used by the Proposed MAFF-Net, by the Restored MAFF-Net, and by every ablated configuration, the features entering the fusion pathway are identical across those configurations. Differences in their behaviour can accordingly be attributed to the modules under study rather than to differences in feature extraction, which is what permits the ablation of Section V-C to be read as an assessment of the components themselves. An architecture in which the backbone were also adapted would not support that inference as directly.

These advantages should not be overstated. The present experiments do not include a configuration in which the encoder is fine-tuned, and the comparative merits of freezing and fine-tuning are therefore not assessed here; the rationale for freezing rests on the considerations above rather than on evidence produced by this study. It should further be recalled, as noted in Section V-A, that the Paper-faithful Reconstruction was trained without pretrained encoder weights, so the comparison between it and the remaining members of the family does not isolate the effect of the pretrained backbone.

VI-D. Limitations

Several limitations qualify the findings of this work and delimit the conclusions that may properly be drawn from them.

Each configuration reported in this study was trained once, with a single experimental seed, and no configuration was repeated. The differences between closely ranked models, and in particular those separating the members of the MAFF-Net family and the four ablated configurations, are small in relation to the per-image dispersion of the measures concerned. It is consequently not possible, on the present evidence, to distinguish these differences from the variation that would arise between repeated runs of the same configuration. Related to this, no statistical analysis was performed: no confidence intervals were estimated and no hypothesis tests were conducted, and no claim of statistical significance is made anywhere in this work. Repeated runs across several seeds, accompanied by an appropriate interval estimate, would be required before the smaller differences reported here could be regarded as established.

The supervision strategy described in Section III-H was active in every configuration reported in this study, and no ablation of its constituent terms was performed. The contribution of the boundary-focused supervision terms to the results is therefore unmeasured, and no claim is advanced regarding their individual efficacy. The component ablation of Section V-C addresses the three architectural modules alone, and was moreover conducted on a single dataset, so the consistency of its findings across the other two benchmarks is not established.

The evaluation is confined to three dermoscopic benchmarks drawn from the same challenge series. All images derive from a single imaging modality and a common annotation protocol, and the generality of the findings to other modalities, to other acquisition devices, or to patient populations differing from those represented in these collections has not been assessed. The comparison is further restricted to models trained and evaluated within this study; no comparison is drawn against results reported in the literature, and several methods discussed in Section II were not implemented here.

Finally, the relative standing of the members of the MAFF-Net family should be stated plainly. The Restored MAFF-Net records the higher value on the majority of the measures reported in Sections V-A and V-B, including the Dice coefficient on all three datasets and the two distance measures on all three datasets. The evidence assembled here therefore does not establish the proposed configuration as the strongest member of the family on these benchmarks, and the contribution of this work is more accurately expressed as the isolation and assessment of three components whose effect is confined to boundary fidelity, together with the evaluation methodology required to observe that effect. The qualitative observations of Section V-D rest on a small number of validation-split examples and are illustrative only.
