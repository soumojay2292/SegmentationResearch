The quantitative measures reported above summarise agreement over entire test subsets and do not indicate the manner in which the predicted contours depart from the reference annotations. Fig. 4 accordingly presents representative segmentation results on ISIC 2018 for the baseline networks and for the members of the MAFF-Net family. The same images and the same reference annotations are used for every method, so that the predictions may be compared directly. The cases shown are illustrative examples selected to span the difficulties characteristic of dermoscopic imaging, and they are intended to complement rather than to substantiate the quantitative findings.

A characteristic common to all evaluated methods concerns the fine structure of the contour. The reference annotations exhibit pixel-level serration along the lesion margin, whereas the predictions of every method, baseline and MAFF-Net variant alike, are comparatively smooth at that scale. None of the evaluated models reproduces the finest irregularities of the manual delineation, and the differences observed between methods therefore concern the coarser shape of the contour rather than its fine detail. This observation applies uniformly and should be borne in mind when the visual results are assessed.

For lesions of low contrast and diffuse margin, in which the pigmented region fades gradually into the surrounding skin, the methods differ chiefly in the degree to which the predicted contour follows the outline of the reference. The baseline networks recover the overall extent of such lesions, but their contours tend towards rounded and approximately convex forms. This tendency is most evident in the predictions of the TransUNet, which reproduces the lesion as a compact convex region and does not follow the concavities present in the reference outline. The predictions of the Restored and Proposed MAFF-Net follow the asymmetric form of the reference more closely in these cases, reproducing indentations along the margin and the elongation of the lesion towards one side, although, as noted above, their contours remain smoother than the annotation at the finest scale.

The methods also differ in the internal consistency of the predicted regions. The predictions of the convolutional baselines contain, in several of the cases examined, small unsegmented cavities within the lesion interior and isolated fragments detached from the principal region along the margin, giving the boundary a broken appearance in places. The predictions of the MAFF-Net family in the same cases form closed regions without interior cavities, and the lesion is returned as a single connected component with a continuous margin. The contours produced by the MAFF-Net variants are correspondingly more regular, in the sense of exhibiting fewer isolated fragments and fewer discontinuities, rather than in the sense of being more detailed.

The cases containing structures adjacent to the target lesion are the most discriminating of those examined. In one such case the dermoscopic image is traversed by dense hair and contains a second, darker pigmented structure near the image border which the reference annotation excludes. The U-Net, the UNet++, and the Attention U-Net each segment this adjacent structure as an additional region, and the TransUNet segments it and further connects it to the target lesion, returning the two as one continuous region. The Restored MAFF-Net and the Proposed MAFF-Net confine the segmentation to the target lesion and return its margin as a closed contour; a residual response to the adjacent structure remains in both, larger in the prediction of the Restored MAFF-Net and reduced to a small isolated fragment in that of the Proposed MAFF-Net. In the same case the dense hair overlying the lesion does not visibly fragment the contours produced by the MAFF-Net variants, whereas the margins produced by several of the baselines are locally irregular in the regions the hair traverses.

These observations are subject to clear limitations. They rest on a small number of saved examples per model, drawn from the validation split rather than from the test subsets on which the quantitative results were computed, and they derive from single training runs. They are consequently illustrative of the behaviour of the models on the cases shown and are not a representative sample, nor do they constitute independent evidence of the differences reported in Sections V-A to V-C. The visual comparison is presented for interpretive value only, and the quantitative measures remain the basis for the comparisons drawn in this work.
