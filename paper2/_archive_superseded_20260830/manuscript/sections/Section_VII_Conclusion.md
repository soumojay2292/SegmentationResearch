# Section VII. Conclusion

> Standalone extract of Section VII from paper2/manuscript/Paper2_Manuscript.md.

---


The delineation of the lesion margin is the principal difficulty of dermoscopic segmentation, and it is the aspect of the task least well served by the region-overlap criteria with which segmentation methods are conventionally optimised and assessed. This work has pursued a targeted refinement of the MAFF-Net framework directed at that aspect. The pretrained encoder, the convolutional decoder, and the auxiliary reconstruction branch are retained from the Original MAFF-Net, and the redesign is confined to the pathway in which spatial and spectral information are combined. In place of a single fusion route, the multi-scale features produced by the frozen backbone are processed along two parallel streams: a spatial stream that preserves regional and contextual structure, and a parameter-free Frequency Branch that provides an explicit spectral route for boundary-sensitive content. A Feature Alignment Module reconciles the statistics of the two representations, and a Gated Fusion Module combines them by a learned, spatially varying weighting. Training is conducted under a region objective supplemented by auxiliary boundary-focused supervision.

The method was evaluated on three public dermoscopic benchmarks against four segmentation architectures trained and assessed under an identical protocol, using region-based and boundary-based measures in combination. All members of the MAFF-Net family exceeded every baseline on both families of measures across all three datasets. A leave-one-out ablation of the three proposed components established that disabling any one of them degrades all three boundary measures simultaneously, while leaving the region measures substantially unaffected; the complete model did not record the highest value on any region measure. The improvements associated with these components are therefore confined to the fidelity of the predicted contour, and the results do not support, and this work does not advance, any claim that the components improve region overlap. It should further be recorded that the proposed configuration was not the strongest member of the MAFF-Net family on these benchmarks, the restored variant recording the higher value on the majority of the measures reported.

Stated conservatively, the contribution of this work is threefold: the formulation of a dual-stream fusion pathway comprising the three components described above; the isolation of each component through a controlled ablation; and the demonstration that their effect is observable in boundary-based measures but not in region-overlap measures, which carries the methodological implication that region-overlap criteria alone are insufficient to characterise methods intended to act upon the lesion margin. No comparison was drawn against results reported in the literature, and no claim of state-of-the-art performance is made.

The principal limitations of the study indicate the directions in which it should be extended. Every configuration was trained once, with a single experimental seed, and no statistical analysis was undertaken; repetition across several seeds, with appropriate interval estimates, is required before the smaller differences reported here can be regarded as established, and this is the most immediate priority. The evaluation is confined to three benchmarks drawn from a single imaging modality and annotation protocol, so assessment on further dermoscopic collections, and on other modalities and acquisition conditions, is needed to determine the generality of the findings. Finally, the boundary-focused supervision terms were active in every configuration and were not ablated, so their individual contribution remains unmeasured; ablating these terms, and examining alternative boundary-supervision formulations and their interaction with the architectural components, is a natural continuation of the present study.
